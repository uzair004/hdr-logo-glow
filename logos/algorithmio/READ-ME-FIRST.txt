algorithm.io — glowing logo files
=================================

Our logo can be made to shine far brighter than normal white on recent Apple screens
(MacBook Pro, Pro Display XDR, iPhone 12 and newer). On every other screen it looks
completely ordinary. This folder has both ways of doing it, at two intensities.

Pick ONE file and upload it as the LinkedIn company page logo.


THE TWO OPTIONS
---------------

  option-1-shape-glows/     The white shape lights up. The square stays black.
                            9.5% of the tile glows. Reads like a neon sign.
                            On an ordinary screen this is our logo exactly as it is
                            today — nobody sees a change.
                            >>> RECOMMENDED <<<

  option-2-square-glows/    The square lights up. The shape becomes a black cut-out.
                            91% of the tile glows. Far more noticeable in a feed.
                            This is what Haladir does.
                            But on an ordinary screen our logo becomes a WHITE square,
                            which is a permanent brand change for most people.


INSIDE EACH FOLDER
------------------

  ...-STRONG.jpg            The level Haladir and the others use. Start here.
  ...-MAXIMUM.jpg           As far as the format goes. On today's screens this looks
                            almost the same as STRONG but clips flat instead of
                            rolling off, which reads as a mistake. Rarely worth it.
  ...-STRONG.png            Same, for our own website.
  ...-MAXIMUM.png           Same, for our own website.
  ...-no-glow.jpg           Plain version, no effect. Re-upload to undo, in 30 seconds.

  option 2 also has:
  ...-STRONG-textured.jpg   Adds a very slow, soft variation across the lit area.
                            It stops the square reading as a flat blown-out slab and
                            makes it look like lit material instead. Haladir's logo
                            has this. It does mean inventing a texture our logo has
                            never had, so it is a real design decision, not a setting.


WHICH FILE FORMAT GOES WHERE
----------------------------

  .jpg    LinkedIn and any other social platform.
          They re-encode uploads but keep the colour profile, which is where the whole
          effect lives. Use the JPEG there.

  .png    Our own website. Lossless and much smaller. Carries the same instruction in
          a different form that social platforms would strip.


BEFORE YOU UPLOAD
-----------------

Check the file in Chrome or Safari first, on a recent MacBook Pro, with Low Power Mode
off and the window in front. If it does not glow there, something stripped it in
transit — re-copy from this folder rather than from a Slack or email preview.

Do not screenshot it to show someone. Screenshots flatten it back to normal white.
Send them the file, or the decision page link.


IF IT LOOKS THE SAME AS THE NORMAL VERSION
------------------------------------------

Open  diagnostics/VERIFY.html  in Chrome or Safari. Not Finder, not Preview.

Section 1 is four flat squares of pure light next to plain white. If those do not
clearly outshine the white swatch, nothing is wrong with the files — it is the screen
or its settings. In order of how often it is the cause:

  1. Screen brightness at 100%. Drop it to about 70%. At full brightness the panel is
     already at its limit and has nothing left in reserve. This is usually the answer.
  2. Low Power Mode is on. It removes the extra range completely.
  3. The window is in the background. Background windows lose the extra brightness.
  4. You are looking in Finder, Quick Look or Preview. Only browsers show it.
  5. You are on an external monitor that is not a Pro Display XDR.

If the flat squares DO outshine the white, the files are working and what you are
seeing is honest: option 1 lights only 9.5% of the square. Section 4 of VERIFY.html
puts option 1, option 2 and Haladir's real file side by side at the same setting.


WORTH KNOWING
-------------

This can stop working. LinkedIn can switch it off site-wide with a small change, and
Slack already has. Treat it as something that runs for a few months, not as our
permanent logo. Nothing breaks when it does — it goes back to looking normal.

It will also annoy some people. A bright patch in a night-time feed is genuinely
uncomfortable, and worse for anyone with migraine or light sensitivity. Option 1 keeps
the bright area small, which is most of why it is the gentler choice.
