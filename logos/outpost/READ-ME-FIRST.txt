Outpost — glowing logo files
============================

The Outpost logo can be made to shine far brighter than normal white on recent Apple
screens (MacBook Pro, Pro Display XDR, iPhone 12 and newer). On every other screen it
looks completely ordinary.

Pick ONE file and upload it as the LinkedIn company page logo.


THIS LOGO IS DIFFERENT FROM THE ALGORITHM.IO ONE
------------------------------------------------

The algorithm.io mark is white on black, so the choice there was which half lights up.
Outpost is a white flower on a blue square with no black at all, so the choice here is
different: how much of the blue comes up with the flower.

The blue is left exactly as it is in option 1. It has NOT been shifted, resaturated or
recoloured — the exact value 106/145/227 survives the conversion unchanged. Verified.


THE TWO OPTIONS
---------------

  option-1-petals-glow/           Only the white flower lights up. The blue stays at
                                  precisely the brightness it is today.
                                  28% of the tile glows. The flower reads as cut from
                                  light, sitting on the blue we already use.
                                  >>> RECOMMENDED <<<

  option-2-whole-tile-backlit/    The blue is lifted too, to roughly the brightness of
                                  normal page white, and the flower goes far above it.
                                  The whole square reads as a lit panel rather than a
                                  flower sitting on a background.
                                  Bolder. Also a brighter block of blue in someone's
                                  feed at midnight, which is the cost.

WHICH FILE FORMAT GOES WHERE
----------------------------

  .jpg    LinkedIn and any other social platform. They re-encode uploads but keep the
          colour profile, which is where the whole effect lives.

  .png    Our own website. Lossless, and carries the same instruction in a different
          form that social platforms would strip.


BEFORE YOU UPLOAD
-----------------

Check the file in Chrome or Safari first, on a recent MacBook Pro, with Low Power Mode
off and the window in front. If it does not glow there, something stripped it in
transit — re-copy from this folder rather than from a Slack or email preview.

Do not screenshot it to show someone. Screenshots flatten it back to normal white.

Check it on a LIGHT background too. LinkedIn's default is light mode, and a blue tile
on white behaves differently from a blue tile on dark. Section 5 of the verify page
does this comparison.


IF IT LOOKS THE SAME AS THE NORMAL VERSION
------------------------------------------

Open  diagnostics/VERIFY.html  in Chrome or Safari. Not Finder, not Preview.

Section 1 is flat squares of pure light next to plain white. If those do not clearly
outshine the white swatch, nothing is wrong with the files — it is the screen or its
settings. In order of how often it is the cause:

  1. Screen brightness at 100%. Drop it to about 70%. At full brightness the panel is
     already at its limit and has nothing left in reserve. This is usually the answer.
  2. Low Power Mode is on. It removes the extra range completely.
  3. The window is in the background. Background windows lose the extra brightness.
  4. You are looking in Finder, Quick Look or Preview. Only browsers show it.
  5. You are on an external monitor that is not a Pro Display XDR.


WORTH KNOWING
-------------

This can stop working. LinkedIn can switch it off site-wide with a small change, and
Slack already has. Treat it as something that runs for a few months, not as our
permanent logo. Nothing breaks when it does — it goes back to looking normal.

It will also annoy some people. A bright patch in a night-time feed is genuinely
uncomfortable, and worse for anyone with migraine or light sensitivity. Option 1 keeps
the lit area to the flower only, which is most of why it is the gentler choice.
